Offer rates, 2026

At Anthropic, about 4 in 100 candidates get an offer. The typical company says yes to half.

Fifty employers convert 20 percent of reported interviews or fewer. Most are AI labs, trading firms or venture-backed startups, and most pay near the top of the market.

Source: Dataford, “Top 50 Companies That Almost Never Make an Offer.” Data snapshot Sept. 4, 2026. Download the table (CSV)

Of the 550 candidates who described an Anthropic interview in the reports Dataford collects, about 4 percent said it ended in an offer. At the median company in the same data, the figure is about 53 percent.

Anthropic heads a list of 50 companies that almost never say yes. Each has at least 300 interview reports, and each converts 20 percent or fewer of them. Hudson River Trading (6 percent), Clipboard Health (7 percent), Canonical (8 percent) and Jane Street (8 percent) come next. OpenAI is sixth, at 9 percent.

The ranking comes from Dataford’s study of companies that almost never make an offer, which also reports difficulty, candidate experience and pay for every company. This page reproduces the table so it can be sorted, cited and downloaded.

Three kinds of company

The list is not a random draw. Two frontier AI labs, Anthropic and OpenAI, sit near the top. Quantitative trading firms fill much of the rest: Hudson River Trading, Jane Street, Akuna, Two Sigma, Citadel, Citadel Securities, SIG, DRW, Virtu and Flow. The third group is well-funded consumer and fintech companies, including Ramp, Plaid, Rippling, DoorDash, Duolingo and Etsy.

Big Tech mostly stays off it. Google, Meta, Amazon, Apple and Microsoft each convert more than 20 percent of reported interviews. The exceptions are NVIDIA, at 16 percent, and Netflix and Pinterest, at 18 percent.

Selective is not the same as difficult

Read the difficulty column against the offer rate and the link weakens as you go down. The first eight companies pair low odds with loops candidates rate 3.23 or harder on a 1-to-5 scale. BairesDev, ninth, converts 12 percent with a 2.72 rating, easier than average. From about the 30th row on, difficulty settles between 2.9 and 3.1.

At those companies, the post argues, the constraint is headcount rather than the bar, so more preparation moves a candidate’s odds less than it would elsewhere.

The experience columns split the list another way. Jane Street converts 8 percent of candidates, yet 70 percent describe the process as positive and 6 percent as negative. Clipboard Health converts 7 percent, and 71 percent of its candidates call the experience negative. Both reject nearly everyone. Only one leaves candidates satisfied.

The pay that comes with a no

Where Dataford has pay data, the low-offer companies sit near the top of the market: Anthropic at a $555,000 median across tech roles, Netflix at $514,000, OpenAI at $468,000, Stripe at $437,000 and Plaid at $370,000. Many trading firms do not publish pay bands, so their pay cells are empty.

How the numbers work

Offer rate is the share of interview reports that were fully parsed, include an outcome and end in an offer. Dataford excluded companies with zero recorded offers as data gaps rather than ranking them. Reports are write-ups submitted by candidates, which Dataford gathers from Glassdoor, Blind, LeetCode and other sources. People who write up an interview are not a random sample of everyone who sat one, so treat the rates as indicators, not as any one candidate’s odds.

Pay is the company median across tech-role pay bands: leveled total compensation where a company publishes a ladder, otherwise the best-supported posted range. Posted ranges understate equity-heavy packages. Full method notes are at the end of the original post.
