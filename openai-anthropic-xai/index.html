xAI pays the most and hires the most. Anthropic is where employees are happiest.

Candidates rate the three labs’ interviews almost identically. On pay, odds and workplace ratings, the three companies land in very different places.

Source: Dataford, “OpenAI vs Anthropic vs xAI: What Is the Best Company to Join?” Data snapshot Sept. 4, 2026. Scorecard CSV

On interview difficulty, candidates barely tell the three best-known frontier labs apart. They rate OpenAI’s process 3.23 out of 5, Anthropic’s 3.28 and xAI’s 3.22.

Almost everything else separates them. Dataford’s head-to-head of OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI lines up 13 signals drawn from interview reports, employee ratings and published pay. Anthropic leads on how employees rate the workplace. xAI leads on pay and on a candidate’s odds. OpenAI sits between the two on most rows.

xAI: the best odds, the weakest ratings

xAI publishes one pay figure, $645,000, for software, machine learning and AI engineers alike. It converts 23 percent of reported interviews, and 51 percent of its candidates describe a positive experience, the highest of the three. Its software engineer loop converts 29 percent, on 50 reports, more than three times OpenAI’s rate for the same role.

It also leaves the most candidates hanging. Seven percent of xAI interview reports describe never hearing back, against 3 percent at OpenAI and at Anthropic. Employees rate xAI lowest of the three on work-life balance (3.3), career growth (3.3) and overall (3.7).

Anthropic: the best-rated workplace, the hardest door

Anthropic’s employees give it 3.8 for work-life balance, 4.7 for career growth and 4.5 overall, the top marks of the three. Those scores rest on 44 employee ratings, a sample the post calls indicative.

For candidates it is the toughest of the three: a 4 percent offer rate across 550 reports, and 38 percent of candidates describing a negative experience. Its ML engineer loop is rated 4.00. Outside software and ML engineering, none of the Anthropic roles with 10 or more reports recorded an offer, though each of those rows rests on 10 to 26 reports.

OpenAI: in the middle, with one exception

OpenAI’s median tech pay is $468,338. Its published ladder runs from $302,500 for security engineers to $536,250 for mobile engineers, with software engineers at $529,000. It converts 9 percent of reported interviews.

The exception is machine learning engineering. OpenAI’s ML engineer interview is rated 3.89, near the top of the difficulty scale, yet 33 percent of the 29 candidates who reported on it received an offer. That is the highest offer rate for any engineering role at the three labs in Dataford’s table.

How the numbers work

Interview samples are 831 reports for OpenAI, 550 for Anthropic and 129 for xAI; employee-rating samples are 130, 44 and 84. Pay figures marked as ladders are leveled total compensation including equity. Anthropic’s median rests on a single disclosed figure, a $555,000 posted range for research engineers. Candidate reports are self-selected. The complete scorecard, pay by role and interview rows are in the original comparison.
