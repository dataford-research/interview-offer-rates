Where to Look for Your First Software Engineer Job in 2026

By Amney Mounir

When people ask me where to apply for a first software engineering job, they usually list the same handful of Big Tech names. Those companies are worth a shot, but they are also the hardest loops to pass, and they say no to most candidates. If your goal is to get the first offer and start building experience, the data points somewhere else.

Dataford ranked 25 companies for a first software engineer job using interview difficulty, offer rate, positive candidate experience, work-life balance and career growth. The full table is in The Best Companies for Your First Software Engineer Job (2026). Here is how I would use it.

Defense, aerospace and national labs lead the list

The entire top 10 is defense, aerospace or a national lab, and 14 of the 25 companies are in that sector. Lockheed Martin is first with a score of 96 out of 100. Its interview is rated 2.54 out of 5, 63% of reported interviews end in an offer, and employees rate work-life balance 4.4.

  • Northrop Grumman is second at 92 and has the easiest interview on the list at 2.37.
  • General Dynamics Mission Systems is third at 91, with a 71% offer rate.
  • Draper, Johns Hopkins APL, Sandia, RTX, Moog, KBR and L3Harris round out the top 10.

These employers hire engineers in volume, run loops that are easier than average, and convert a large share of interviews into offers. Twenty of the 25 companies turn more than half of reported interviews into offers, and nothing in the top 25 is rated harder than 2.91.

What you give up

Pay is the trade. The companies on this list pay between about $82k and $173k, and most sit between $100k and $160k. The median row is about $130k. Johns Hopkins APL is the best paid in the top 10 at about $173k, and Ericsson, GDIT, Appfolio and Draper are all above $150k.

That is well below what Big Tech and AI labs pay. In exchange, you get a loop that is much more likely to say yes, a company employees rate well on work-life, and real production experience you can take to your next interview.

A few names outside defense

If defense work is not for you, the list still has options. Sopra Steria has the best offer odds in the group at 73%. Capgemini, Fujitsu, Boston Scientific, Motorola Solutions, Aveva, Appfolio and Ericsson all make the top 25 with easier than average interviews.

How I would plan the search

  1. Pick five companies from this list and five stretch companies you really want.
  2. Apply to the accessible group first so your first real interviews are the ones with the best odds. You will be sharper by the time the stretch loops start.
  3. For each company, read what candidates were actually asked and how many rounds they went through.
  4. Practice coding problems and a short project walkthrough out loud. Easier loops still expect you to explain your thinking clearly.

Dataford's software engineer interview hub has real questions and company guides for the role, so you can see how Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman or Sopra Steria run their process before you apply.

A good first job gives you real experience and a team that teaches you. The companies on this list score well on both, and they are far more likely to make you an offer.
