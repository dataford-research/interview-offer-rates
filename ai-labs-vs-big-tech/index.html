Offer rates, 2026

The AI labs’ interviews are no harder than Google’s. Getting hired is.

Weighted by report volume, candidates rate frontier-lab loops 3.23 out of 5 and Big Tech loops 3.22. Offer rates run from 4 to 23 percent at the labs and from 16 to 35 percent at the big employers.

Source: Dataford, “AI Labs vs Big Tech: Who Has the Hardest Interviews?” Data snapshot Sept. 4, 2026. Company CSV · Role CSV

Among 14 employers in a comparison of frontier AI labs and the largest tech companies, the hardest interview does not belong to OpenAI or Anthropic. It belongs to Google, which candidates rate 3.35 on a 1-to-5 scale, just ahead of Meta at 3.33.

Weighted by the number of reports, the seven labs average 3.23 and the seven big employers 3.22, according to Dataford’s analysis of AI labs versus Big Tech. On the bar itself, the two groups are level.

Where they split

The outcomes are not level. Anthropic converts 4 percent of reported interviews, OpenAI 9 percent, Google DeepMind 18 percent and xAI 23 percent. Groq, at 43 percent, is the only lab above 25 percent, and that figure rests on 53 reports. Among the big employers, Amazon converts 35 percent, Microsoft 32, Apple 29, Google 21 and Meta 20.

Candidates also rate the lab processes less kindly. About a third of reports on OpenAI (34 percent) and Anthropic (36 percent) describe a positive experience, and 27 percent on Mistral AI, compared with 66 percent at Microsoft, 65 percent at Google and 61 percent at Apple. The post reads the low positive rates as a sign that much of the gap is silence rather than a clear no.

The labs do pay for the selectivity. Median tech pay is $645,000 at xAI, $555,000 at Anthropic and $468,000 at OpenAI, against $514,000 at Netflix, $360,000 at Meta and $294,000 at Amazon.

The job title matters more than the logo

For software engineers, the spread between employers is narrow. Databricks (3.45), Google (3.43), Anthropic (3.40), Meta (3.39), OpenAI (3.34), Amazon (3.32) and xAI (3.30) sit within 0.15 points of one another.

Machine learning engineering is another matter. Anthropic’s ML engineer loop is rated 4.00, the highest for any company and role pair in the dataset, and OpenAI’s 3.89. Amazon’s is 2.65. That is a 1.35-point spread for a single job title.

One pairing stands out. Microsoft’s ML engineer interview is rated 3.40, the same as Anthropic’s software engineer loop. Microsoft’s converts 60 percent of candidates; Anthropic’s converts 6 percent. The Microsoft figure rests on 17 reports.

How the numbers work

The comparison covers seven frontier labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, xAI, Groq, Mistral AI, Cohere) and seven large tech employers (Google, Meta, Amazon, NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft, Netflix). Difficulty is the mean of candidate ratings, and the group averages are weighted by report volume. Offer rate counts reports with a parsed outcome. The reports are candidate-submitted, so they describe the people who chose to write up an interview, not every applicant.

Method notes and the original charts are in the full AI labs versus Big Tech post.
